01

Introduction to Accounting

DEFINITION Accounting is the art of recording, classifying and summarising in a significant manner, and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of a financial character, and interpreting the results thereof. (American Institute of Certified Public Accountants — AICPA)

Book-keeping vs Accounting vs Accountancy

Basis Book-keeping Accounting Accountancy
Scope Recording & classifying Recording → summarising → interpreting Whole body of knowledge/principles
Stage Primary / clerical Analytical Conceptual framework
Skill Lower level Higher, needs judgement Highest
Relation Begins the process Begins where book-keeping ends Governs both

Objectives & functions

  • Maintain systematic records of financial transactions.
  • Ascertain profit or loss (via Trading and P&L account).
  • Ascertain financial position (via Balance Sheet).
  • Provide information to users for decision-making.
  • Act as legal evidence and help meet statutory/tax obligations.

Branches of accounting

Branch Focus
Financial accounting Historical records; profit & financial position for external users.
Cost accounting Ascertaining and controlling cost of products/services.
Management accounting Internal reports to help managers plan, decide and control.

Users of accounting information

INTERNAL Owners, management, employees.
EXTERNAL Investors, lenders/banks, creditors, government & tax authorities, researchers, public.

Basis of accounting

Cash basis Accrual (mercantile) basis
Records only when cash is received/paid. Records when income is earned / expense is incurred, regardless of cash.
Ignores outstanding & prepaid items. Considers outstanding, prepaid, accrued items.
Not recognised by Companies Act. Legally required for companies; gives a true picture.
REMEMBER

Accounting records only monetary transactions — quality of management, staff morale, etc. are important but not recorded (money measurement concept).

02

Basic Accounting Terms

These recur in every chapter and in objective questions — fix the exact meanings.

Term Meaning
Capital Amount invested by the owner; a liability of the business towards the owner.
Drawings Cash/goods withdrawn by owner for personal use; reduces capital.
Assets Resources owned that give future benefit — fixed, current, tangible, intangible, fictitious.
Liabilities Amounts owed by the business — internal (capital) & external (creditors, loans).
Debtor Person who owes money to the business.
Creditor Person to whom the business owes money.
Revenue / income Inflow from sale of goods, services, interest, commission, etc.
Expense Cost incurred to earn revenue (rent, salary, wages).
Expenditure Spending of money — capital (long-term benefit) vs revenue (short-term).
Purchases / Sales Goods bought / sold for resale (cash or credit).
Stock (Inventory) Goods unsold — opening & closing stock.
Goods Items in which the business regularly deals.
Voucher Documentary evidence of a transaction.
MCQ TRAP

Fictitious assets are not real assets — they are unwritten-off expenses/losses (e.g. preliminary expenses, discount on issue of shares). Intangible assets (goodwill, patents) are real and have value.

03

Accounting Principles — GAAP

GAAP = Generally Accepted Accounting Principles: the common rules that make statements uniform and comparable. They split into concepts (assumptions) and conventions (practices).

Accounting concepts

Concept Core idea
Business entity Business is separate from its owner; owner's capital is a liability of the business.
Money measurement Only transactions expressible in money are recorded.
Going concern Business will continue for the foreseeable future; assets recorded at cost, not break-up value.
Accounting period Life is split into fixed periods (usually 1 year) to measure results.
Cost concept Assets recorded at acquisition (historical) cost.
Dual aspect Every transaction has two effects — basis of double entry. Assets = Liabilities + Capital.
Matching Match expenses of a period with revenues of the same period to find true profit.
Realisation Revenue is recognised when it is earned/realised, not when cash is received.
Accrual Record income/expense when it arises, irrespective of cash flow.
Objectivity Records must be based on verifiable evidence (vouchers).

Accounting conventions

Convention Core idea
Consistency Same methods followed year to year → comparability.
Conservatism (Prudence) “Anticipate no profit, provide for all possible losses.” Closing stock at cost or market, whichever is lower.
Materiality Record/disclose only items significant enough to affect decisions.
Full disclosure All relevant information must be disclosed truthfully.
AS-1 · FUNDAMENTAL ASSUMPTIONS

Under Accounting Standard 1, three assumptions are presumed unless stated otherwise: Going concern, Consistency, Accrual. If followed, no disclosure needed; if not followed, disclosure is required.

MCQ TRAP

Valuing closing stock at “cost or market price, whichever is lower” is an application of Conservatism / Prudence — a very common one-liner question.

04

The Accounting Equation

Flowing from the dual aspect concept, total assets always equal total claims against them.

Assets = Liabilities + Capital
Capital = Assets − Liabilities
Capital = Opening Capital + Profit + Fresh Capital − Drawings

Effect of transactions (illustration)

Transaction Assets Liabilities Capital
Started business with cash ₹1,00,000 +1,00,000 0 +1,00,000
Bought goods on credit ₹20,000 +20,000 +20,000 0
Paid creditor ₹5,000 −5,000 −5,000 0
Owner withdrew ₹2,000 −2,000 0 −2,000
KEY

Whatever the transaction, the equation must stay balanced. If it doesn't, an error exists.

05

Double Entry & Golden Rules

DOUBLE ENTRY SYSTEM Every transaction affects two accounts — one debited, one credited — with equal amounts. Total debits always equal total credits.

Advantages: complete record, arithmetical accuracy checked via trial balance, easy to detect fraud/errors, reliable profit and position.

Traditional (British) classification of accounts

Type Covers Golden rule
Personal Persons, firms, institutions (Ram's A/c, Bank A/c, Capital A/c) Dr the receiver, Cr the giver
Real Assets & property (Cash, Machinery, Building) Dr what comes in, Cr what goes out
Nominal Expenses, losses, incomes, gains (Rent, Salary, Interest received) Dr all expenses & losses, Cr all incomes & gains

Modern (American) approach — rules by nature of account

Account Increase Decrease
Assets Debit Credit
Expenses / Losses Debit Credit
Liabilities Credit Debit
Capital Credit Debit
Income / Gains Credit Debit
MNEMONIC

“DEAD CLIC” → Debit increases Expenses, Assets, Drawings · Credit increases Liabilities, Income, Capital.

Journal — the Book of Original Entry

The journal records transactions chronologically before posting to the ledger. The process is called journalising.

Journal format
Date Particulars L.F. Dr (₹) Cr (₹)
Apr 1 Cash A/c … Dr
   To Capital A/c
(Being business started with cash) 		— 1,00,000 —

  • Narration — the brief explanation in brackets under each entry.
  • Compound entry — one entry involving more than two accounts.
  • L.F. (Ledger Folio) — page number where the entry is posted in the ledger.
07

Ledger — the Book of Final Entry

The ledger is the principal book that contains all accounts. Transferring entries from journal to ledger is posting. A “T-account” has a debit (left) and credit (right) side.

Cash Account
Dr
To Capital A/c1,00,000
Cr
By Purchases A/c20,000
By Balance c/d80,000

Balancing: the difference between the two sides is the closing balance (balance c/d), carried to the next period as balance b/d. Assets/expenses normally have a debit balance; liabilities/capital/income a credit balance.

08

Trial Balance

DEFINITION A statement (not an account) listing debit and credit balances of all ledger accounts on a given date, prepared to test the arithmetical accuracy of the books.

Objectives: check arithmetic accuracy · basis for final accounts · summary of all accounts · helps locate errors.

Errors that a trial balance can & cannot detect

NOT DISCLOSED (agrees despite error)

Errors of omission (complete) · errors of principle · errors of commission that are compensating · recording wrong amount in both accounts · posting to wrong account of the same class.

DISCLOSED (trial balance disagrees)

Posting to wrong side · posting wrong amount to one account · omitting to post one side · wrong casting (totalling) · errors in carrying forward.

MCQ TRAP

A trial balance that tallies is not a proof of complete accuracy — errors of principle and complete omission still leave it balanced.

09

Subsidiary Books & Cash Book

When transactions are many, the journal is split into special/subsidiary books for speed and division of labour.

Subsidiary book Records
Purchases book Credit purchases of goods only.
Sales book Credit sales of goods only.
Purchase returns (returns outward) Goods returned to suppliers.
Sales returns (returns inward) Goods returned by customers.
Bills receivable / payable book Bills of exchange received / accepted.
Cash book All cash & bank transactions.
Journal proper Entries not fitting any special book (opening, closing, adjustments).

Types of cash book

  • Single column — cash only.
  • Double column — cash + bank (or cash + discount).
  • Triple column — cash, bank and discount columns.
  • Petty cash book — small routine payments, usually on the imprest system (a fixed float is reimbursed to the original amount each period).
DUAL NATURE

The cash book is both a subsidiary book and a ledger account — cash/bank accounts are not separately opened in the ledger.

MCQ TRAP

Contra entry = a transaction affecting both cash and bank columns (e.g. cash deposited into bank), marked with the letter “C”; it is not posted to the ledger again.

10

Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

DEFINITION A statement reconciling the balance shown by the firm's cash book (bank column) with the balance shown by the pass book / bank statement on a particular date.

Common causes of difference

  • Cheques issued but not yet presented for payment.
  • Cheques deposited but not yet cleared/credited.
  • Bank charges, interest debited by bank not entered in cash book.
  • Interest/dividends collected by bank directly.
  • Direct deposits by customers; standing-order payments by bank.
  • Errors in the cash book or the pass book.
APPROACH

Start from one balance, then add/subtract each item according to how it affects that book, to arrive at the other balance. Remember: an overdraft is a credit balance in the cash book and a debit balance in the pass book.

11

Depreciation, Provisions & Reserves

DEPRECIATION The permanent, continuous and gradual fall in the book value of a fixed asset due to use, wear & tear, obsolescence or efflux of time. It is a non-cash expense charged against profit.

Causes: wear and tear, passage of time, obsolescence, depletion, accidents.

Straight-line vs Written-down-value method

Basis Straight Line (SLM / Fixed Instalment) Written Down Value (WDV / Diminishing)
Charged on Original cost Book value (reducing balance)
Amount each year Equal every year Decreases every year
Book value Can become zero Never becomes exactly zero
Suitable for Assets with uniform use (furniture, patents) Assets needing higher early write-off (machinery, vehicles)
Recognised by — Income Tax Act (mainly)
SLM p.a. = (Cost − Scrap value) ÷ Useful life

Provision vs Reserve

Provision Reserve
For a known liability of uncertain amount (e.g. provision for doubtful debts). Appropriation of profit to strengthen the business.
Charge against profit (compulsory). Appropriation of profit (after profit is found).
Created even if there is a loss. Created only if there are profits.
Not available for dividend. Can be used for dividend (revenue reserve).
MCQ TRAP

Reserve vs Provision: provision reduces profit and is a charge; reserve is created from profit. Also distinguish revenue reserve (distributable) from capital reserve (not for dividend).

12

Rectification of Errors

Error type Meaning
Error of omission Transaction fully or partly not recorded.
Error of commission Wrong amount, wrong posting, wrong casting, wrong balancing.
Error of principle Ignoring accounting principles — e.g. treating capital expenditure as revenue.
Compensating error Two or more errors that cancel each other's effect.
SUSPENSE ACCOUNT

When a trial balance does not agree and the difference cannot be located before final accounts, it is temporarily placed in a suspense account. As one-sided errors are rectified, the suspense account is cleared; when it shows nil, all such errors are corrected.

13

Final Accounts

Prepared at year-end to know profit/loss and financial position. Order: Trading A/c → Profit & Loss A/c → Balance Sheet.

1 · Trading Account

Finds Gross Profit (or gross loss) from buying and selling.

Gross Profit = Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold
COGS = Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Stock

Direct expenses: wages, carriage inward, freight, factory rent, power & fuel.

2 · Profit & Loss Account

Starts with gross profit; deducts indirect expenses (office, selling, financial) and adds other incomes to give Net Profit.

Net Profit = Gross Profit + Other Incomes − Indirect Expenses

3 · Balance Sheet

A statement (not an account) of assets and liabilities showing financial position on a date. It always balances (dual aspect). Marshalling = the order of arranging items — by liquidity or by permanence.

Common adjustments

Adjustment Treatment
Closing stock Credit Trading A/c; show as current asset in B/S.
Outstanding expense Add to expense (P&L); show as liability.
Prepaid expense Deduct from expense; show as asset.
Accrued income Add to income; show as asset.
Income received in advance Deduct from income; show as liability.
Depreciation Debit P&L; deduct from asset in B/S.
Bad debts / provision for doubtful debts Debit P&L; deduct from debtors.
GOLDEN CHECK

Every adjustment given outside the trial balance affects two places — one in Trading/P&L, one in the Balance Sheet.

14

Bills of Exchange (Overview)

DEFINITION (NI ACT 1881) An instrument in writing containing an unconditional order, signed by the maker, directing a certain person to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person or the bearer.
  • Drawer — maker (creditor) who draws the bill.
  • Drawee — the person directed to pay (debtor); becomes acceptor after accepting.
  • Payee — the person to receive payment.
  • Days of grace — 3 extra days added to compute the due date.
  • Discounting — getting a bill encashed from a bank before due date at a discount.
  • Endorsement / dishonour / noting — transfer, non-payment, and its official record by a notary.
15

Accounting Standards, Ind AS & IFRS

  • Accounting Standards (AS) — written policy documents issued by the ICAI (Institute of Chartered Accountants of India) to bring uniformity.
  • Ind AS — Indian Accounting Standards converged with IFRS, notified by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; applicable to specified companies in phases.
  • IFRS — International Financial Reporting Standards issued by the IASB, aimed at global comparability.
  • Objectives of standards: uniformity, comparability, reliability, reduced accounting alternatives.
EXAM POINTER

AS in India are issued by ICAI; IFRS by the IASB. Ind AS are “converged with” (not identical to) IFRS.

Ratio Analysis — Essentials

Tools to interpret financial statements. Group and remember the standard ratios.

Type Ratio Formula

Proprietary Shareholders' Funds ÷ Total Assets
Profitability Gross profit ratio (Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100
Net profit ratio (Net Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100
Activity / Turnover Stock turnover COGS ÷ Average Stock
Debtors turnover Net Credit Sales ÷ Average Debtors
17

Cost & Management Accounting (Basics)

  • Elements of cost: Material, Labour, Expenses → each direct or indirect. Indirect items together form overheads.
  • Prime cost = Direct Material + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses.
  • Cost of production = Prime cost + Factory (works) overhead + Office overhead.
  • Behaviour: fixed, variable and semi-variable costs.
  • Marginal costing & break-even: Contribution = Sales − Variable cost; BEP (units) = Fixed cost ÷ Contribution per unit.
  • Management accounting uses this data (plus budgets, standards) purely for internal decisions; it has no fixed statutory format.
Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost
BEP (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit
P/V ratio = (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100
18

Quick Self-Test

Answer in your head, then reveal. Great for the final days before the exam.

Which concept treats the owner and the business as separate?
Business entity concept. Owner's capital is therefore a liability of the business.
Valuing stock at “cost or market price, whichever is lower” follows which convention?
Conservatism / Prudence.
Golden rule for a nominal account?
Debit all expenses & losses, Credit all incomes & gains.
Name two errors a trial balance cannot disclose.
Any two of: error of complete omission, error of principle, compensating error, wrong amount recorded in both accounts, posting to wrong account of the same class.
Under which method does depreciation stay equal every year?
Straight-line (fixed instalment) method, charged on original cost.
Difference between a provision and a reserve in one line?
A provision is a charge against profit for a known liability; a reserve is an appropriation of profit.
Formula for Gross Profit?
Gross Profit = Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold, where COGS = Opening Stock + Net Purchases + Direct Expenses − Closing Stock.
Who issues Accounting Standards in India, and who issues IFRS?
Accounting Standards → ICAI; IFRS → IASB.
Ideal current ratio and quick ratio?
Current ratio 2:1, Quick ratio 1:1.
What is a contra entry?
A transaction affecting both cash and bank columns of the cash book (e.g. cash deposited into bank), marked “C” and not re-posted to the ledger.
