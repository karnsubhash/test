Auditing & Insurance — Comprehensive Notes

UPSC EPFO (APFC / EO-AO) · for the 2026 exam

 

Auditing principles and insurance principles are STATIC (definitional) — the bulk of this subject does not change. The one part with genuine current affairs is the insurance SECTOR (regulator, FDI, new initiatives), covered in Section 3. This is the most scoring-per-hour block: ~20 marks across the last two papers, almost entirely definitional.

1 · Auditing

Nature, objectives & scope

●        Meaning: an independent examination of financial information of any entity to express an opinion on its true and fair view

●        Primary objective: to express an opinion on the truth and fairness of the financial statements

●        Secondary objectives: detection and prevention of errors and frauds

●        Audit does NOT include: preparation of the books of accounts — that is the client's/accountant's job, not the auditor's

●        Errors vs Frauds: errors are unintentional; frauds are intentional (misappropriation of cash/goods, manipulation of accounts / window dressing)

Types of audit

●        By statute: Statutory audit (compulsory by law) vs Voluntary audit

●        By scope/timing: Continuous, Periodical/Final, Interim, Balance-sheet audit

●        Others: Internal audit (by the entity's own staff, for management) vs External/Statutory audit; Cost audit; Management audit; Government audit (CAG)

Audit process — planning to opinion

Term

Meaning

Overall audit strategy (SA 300)

sets the SCOPE, TIMING and DIRECTION of the audit; guides the development of the detailed audit plan

Audit plan

the detailed plan flowing from the strategy — nature, timing and extent of procedures

Audit programme

a detailed written list of the procedures/steps to be performed

Audit documentation (working papers)

record of procedures performed, evidence obtained and conclusions reached; includes the audit programme and summaries of significant matters; may be on paper/electronic. NOTE: an 'audit file' (the folder) is NOT the same as 'audit documentation'

Audit evidence

information used by the auditor to arrive at conclusions; must be sufficient & appropriate

Vouching vs Verification (very high-yield)

Term

Meaning

Vouching

examination of documentary evidence (vouchers) to verify the AUTHENTICITY & validity of recorded transactions — 'the backbone of auditing'

Verification

confirming the EXISTENCE, OWNERSHIP, VALUATION and AUTHORITY of acquisition of the assets & liabilities in the balance sheet. It is NOT about the 'historical accuracy of accounts'

Valuation

a part of verification — checking assets are correctly valued (e.g. depreciation, NRV)

Other key auditing concepts

●        Internal control & internal check: internal check = division of work so that no one person handles a transaction end-to-end; internal control is the wider system

●        Audit risk: risk that the auditor gives an inappropriate opinion; components — inherent, control, detection risk

●        Materiality: information is material if its omission/misstatement could influence users' decisions

●        Types of audit opinion: Unqualified (clean), Qualified, Adverse, Disclaimer of opinion

●        Auditing standards: Standards on Auditing (SAs) issued by ICAI; oversight of auditors of large/listed entities by NFRA

2 · Insurance — Principles & Concepts

The principles of insurance

Principle

Meaning

Utmost Good Faith (uberrimae fidei)

both parties must disclose ALL material facts truthfully

Insurable Interest

the insured must stand to suffer a financial loss from the event insured against

Indemnity

the insurer compensates the ACTUAL loss only — the insured cannot profit

Subrogation

after indemnifying the insured, the insurer steps into the insured's rights to recover the loss from the third party at fault (a corollary of indemnity)

Contribution

where the same risk is insured with more than one insurer, they share the loss proportionately

Proximate Cause (causa proxima)

the nearest/effective cause of the loss determines liability

Mitigation / Loss Minimisation

it is the insured's DUTY to take reasonable steps to minimise the loss or damage

Contracts of indemnity vs non-indemnity

●        Contracts of indemnity: Fire, Marine and Motor insurance — compensate measurable actual loss

●        NOT contracts of indemnity: Life insurance and Personal Accident — the sum assured is fixed; a human life cannot be valued, so indemnity/subrogation/contribution do NOT apply to life insurance

●        Applies to all insurance: Utmost good faith & insurable interest apply to ALL contracts; indemnity, subrogation & contribution apply to general (non-life) insurance

Types & structure of insurance

●        Life insurance: term, endowment, whole-life, ULIP, annuity; based on the principle of a fixed assured sum

●        General (non-life) insurance: fire, marine, motor, health, liability, miscellaneous

●        Reinsurance vs Co-insurance: reinsurance = insurer insures part of its risk with another insurer; co-insurance = two+ insurers jointly cover one risk

●        Key documents: proposal form, cover note, policy, premium; insurable interest must exist (life: at inception; fire: at inception & loss; marine: at time of loss)

3 · Insurance Sector — Current Affairs 2026

The one current-affairs area in this subject. Biggest development: the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Bill, 2025 — passed by Parliament on 16-17 December 2025 — raised the insurance FDI cap to 100%.

Item

Detail

Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Bill, 2025

Passed by Parliament 16-17 Dec 2025; amends the Insurance Act 1938, LIC Act 1956 and IRDAI Act 1999

100% FDI

Foreign investment cap in insurance raised from 74% to 100%

Stronger IRDAI powers

power to disgorge wrongful gains; to set limits on agent commissions/remuneration; rationalised penalties; one-time licensing; suspension (not straight cancellation) of licence

Goal

supports 'Insurance for All by 2047'; deepen penetration; ease of doing business

Bima Sugam

a unified DIGITAL insurance marketplace (DPI for insurance) — scheduled to launch around September 2026; initially motor, health and term products

Bima trinity

Bima Sugam (marketplace) + Bima Vistaar (affordable bundled cover) + Bima Vahak (women-led rural distribution)

Regulator

IRDAI — statutory body under the IRDA Act, 1999; HQ Hyderabad

Flagship govt insurance schemes

PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (life), PM Suraksha Bima Yojana (accident), Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY (health)
