Accountancy — Comprehensive Notes

UPSC EPFO (APFC / EO-AO) · for the 2026 exam

 

Accountancy is a static subject — concepts, conventions, rules and formulas do not change year to year. Questions are Class 11–12 / graduate-level theory plus small calculations. This is the biggest specialist block (≈55 marks across the last two papers). Regulatory context: Accounting Standards (AS) are issued by ICAI; large/listed companies follow Ind AS (converged with IFRS); audit/accounting oversight of large entities lies with NFRA.

1 · Concepts, Conventions & Assumptions

Fundamental accounting assumptions (AS-1)

●        The three: Going Concern · Consistency · Accrual. (If followed, no separate disclosure needed; if not, disclosure is required.)

●        Watch-out: 'Matching' is a CONCEPT, not one of the three fundamental assumptions.

Major considerations for selecting accounting policies (AS-1)

●        The three: Prudence · Substance over Form · Materiality. (Consistency is an assumption, NOT one of these three considerations.)

Accounting concepts

Concept

Meaning

Business Entity

Business is separate from its owner; owner's capital is a liability of the business

Money Measurement

Only transactions measurable in money are recorded

Going Concern

Business will continue indefinitely → assets at book/historical value, not realizable value

Accounting Period

Life split into fixed periods (usually one year) for reporting

Cost (Historical)

Assets recorded at acquisition cost

Dual Aspect

Every transaction has two effects → Assets = Liabilities + Capital

Matching

Expenses matched with the revenues of the same period

Realisation

Revenue recognised when earned/realised, not when cash is received

Accrual

Income & expenses recorded when they accrue, regardless of cash flow

Accounting conventions

Convention

Meaning

Conservatism / Prudence

Anticipate no profit but provide for all possible losses (e.g. stock at lower of cost or NRV; provision for doubtful debts; provision against fall in investment value)

Consistency

Same methods followed period to period for comparability

Full Disclosure

All material facts disclosed in the financial statements

Materiality

Only significant items need separate treatment/disclosure

2 · Types of Accounts & Rules

Account type

Golden rule / examples

Personal (persons/firms)

Debit the receiver, Credit the giver. (Debtors, creditors, capital, outstanding rent = representative personal a/c)

Real (assets)

Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out. (Cash, machinery, GOODWILL = real a/c)

Nominal (expenses/incomes)

Debit all expenses & losses, Credit all incomes & gains. (Salary, rent, commission)

●        Modern (American) approach: Increase in Asset/Expense = Debit; Increase in Liability/Capital/Income = Credit.

●        Key facts: Ledger = principal book of accounts; Journal = book of original/prime entry. Capital account normally has a CREDIT balance.

3 · Capital vs Revenue

Item

Detail / examples

Capital expenditure

Benefit > 1 year; acquiring/improving fixed assets. Freight & installation, legal fees to acquire property, overhaul of a second-hand machine before use, foreign tour to purchase machinery

Revenue expenditure

Benefit within the year; running the business. Repairs, salaries, replacing a worn-out part (restoring capacity), cost merely to remove a worn part

Deferred revenue expenditure

Revenue in nature but benefit spread over years (e.g. heavy advertising)

Capital receipt

Non-recurring, not from operations — e.g. insurance claim for an asset destroyed by fire, sale of fixed asset, capital contributed

Revenue receipt

Recurring, from operations — sales, commission, interest received

4 · Books of Account & Trial Balance

●        Subsidiary books: Purchases book, Sales book, Purchase/Sales returns books, Bills receivable/payable books, Cash book, Journal proper

●        Cash book: Both a journal and a ledger; discount column records CASH discount; cash sales are recorded in the cash book (NOT the sales day book)

●        Trial balance: A list of ledger balances to check arithmetical accuracy; its agreement is NOT conclusive proof of accuracy

●        Suspense account: A temporary account opened when the trial balance does not agree; closed once errors are located

5 · Errors & Rectification

Error type

Meaning

Error of Omission

Transaction wholly (complete) or partly (partial) omitted

Error of Commission

Wrong amount, wrong posting, wrong casting, posting to the wrong account of the same class

Error of Principle

Violates an accounting principle — e.g. capital expenditure treated as revenue (asset purchase booked as an expense)

Compensating Error

Two or more errors that cancel each other out

●        NOT disclosed by the Trial Balance: complete omission, error of principle, compensating errors, and errors of commission where a correct amount is posted to a wrong account on the correct side

●        Disclosed by the Trial Balance: one-sided errors — posting to the wrong side, wrong amount posted to the ledger, omission of one-side posting, wrong casting/carry-forward, wrong balancing

6 · Depreciation, Provisions & Reserves

Depreciation

●        Meaning & causes: systematic allocation of an asset's cost over its useful life; causes: wear & tear, efflux of time, obsolescence, depletion, permanent fall in value

●        Straight Line Method (SLM): fixed amount each year on ORIGINAL cost; book value can reach zero/scrap

●        Written Down Value (WDV): fixed % each year on the reducing BOOK value; higher depreciation in early years

Provisions vs Reserves

●        Provision: a CHARGE against profit (created whether or not there is profit) — e.g. provision for doubtful debts, depreciation, tax

●        Reserve: an APPROPRIATION of profit — Capital reserve (from capital profits, generally not for dividend), Revenue/General reserve (free), specific reserves, secret reserve

●        Key statement: 'Provision is a charge against profit' is correct; a reserve is not a charge

7 · Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

●        Purpose: reconciles the cash-book (bank column) balance with the pass-book balance

●        Common reasons for difference: cheques issued but not yet presented; cheques deposited but not yet cleared; bank charges/interest; direct deposits & standing instructions; errors

8 · Final Accounts & Adjustments

●        Components: Trading A/c (gross profit) → Profit & Loss A/c (net profit) → Balance Sheet (assets = liabilities + capital)

●        Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital

9 · Inventory Valuation (AS-2)

●        Rule: valued at the LOWER of cost or Net Realisable Value (conservatism)

●        Cost includes: cost of purchase + conversion cost + other costs to bring inventory to its present location & condition

●        Cost EXCLUDES: abnormal wastage, storage costs (unless necessary for production), administrative overheads, selling & distribution costs

●        Cost formulas: FIFO (first-in-first-out), Weighted Average. LIFO is used in exam sums but is NOT permitted under AS-2 / Ind AS-2 for reporting

●        Perpetual vs periodic: perpetual = continuous record after each transaction; periodic = physical count at period-end

10 · Bad Debts, Ratios & Key Formulas

Bad debts & provision for doubtful debts

●        Amount charged to P&L: Bad debts written off + New provision − Old (existing) provision

●        Worked pattern: Receivables 25,00,000 − further bad debts 2,00,000 = 23,00,000; new provision @10% = 2,30,000. Charge = (40,000 + 2,00,000) + 2,30,000 − 1,40,000 = ₹1,30,000

Operating profit

●        Formula: Operating Profit = Net Profit + Non-operating Expenses − Non-operating Incomes (= Gross Profit − Operating Expenses + Operating Incomes)

Key accounting ratios

Ratio

Formula (ideal)

Current ratio

Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities (ideal 2:1)

Quick / liquid ratio

(Current Assets − Stock − Prepaid) ÷ Current Liabilities (ideal 1:1)

Debt-Equity ratio

Long-term Debt ÷ Shareholders' Funds

Gross Profit ratio

(Gross Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100

Net Profit ratio

(Net Profit ÷ Net Sales) × 100

Operating ratio

((COGS + Operating Expenses) ÷ Net Sales) × 100

Inventory turnover

COGS ÷ Average Inventory

Return on Capital Employed

(EBIT ÷ Capital Employed) × 100

11 · Other tested areas (brief)

●        Goodwill: an intangible asset → a REAL account; valuation methods: average profit, super profit, capitalisation

●        Bills of exchange: drawer, drawee, payee; terms — days of grace (3), noting charges on dishonour, discounting, endorsement

●        Company accounts basics: share capital (authorised/issued/subscribed/paid-up), shares at premium/discount, debentures; capital reserve vs securities premium

●        Cost concepts: prime cost, factory/works cost, cost of production, total cost; direct vs indirect (overheads); administration overheads are excluded from inventory cost

Note: content is standard Class 11–12 / foundation accounting and does not change year to year, so no 'current affairs' updating is needed for this subject (unlike Labour/Social Security). The only evolving angle is the accounting-standards framework (AS vs Ind AS convergence, NFRA oversight); the APFC paper tests fundamentals. Cross-check any specific figure/format against a standard text (TS Grewal / DK Goel) before the exam.
